    How the numbers work
    • Potential intensity follows Emanuel’s heat-engine theory (Emanuel 1986; Bister & Emanuel 1998): V² = (Ck/Cd) · (Ts − To)/To · Δk, with Ck/Cd = 0.9, near-surface air at 80% humidity and 1 °C cooler than the sea, and a 0.8 reduction from gradient-level to 10 m wind.
    • Realized intensity scales potential intensity down with a ramp from 25.5 to 27.5 °C and a wind-shear penalty above 8 kt. Both factors are illustrative, not a forecast model.
    • Central pressure uses the Atkinson–Holliday (1977) wind–pressure relation. The probe uses a Holland (1980) pressure and wind profile.
    • Heat released scales NOAA HRD’s estimate (1.5 cm/day of rain over a 665 km radius ≈ 6 × 10¹⁴ W) by about 7% per °C of sea temperature and by storm strength. World electricity is taken as ~3.5 TW average (≈ 30,000 TWh in 2024). A Hiroshima bomb is ~6.3 × 10¹³ J.
    • Wind power integrates ρ·Cd·V³ over a Holland wind profile out to 500 km.
    • Surge ranges are the historical Saffir–Simpson values. The NHC dropped them from the scale in 2010 because real surge depends heavily on coastline shape and depth.
    • Sources: NOAA HRD hurricane FAQ, NHC Saffir–Simpson scale, Emanuel, limits on hurricane intensity.
